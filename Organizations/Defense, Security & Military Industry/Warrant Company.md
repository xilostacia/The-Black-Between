**Warrant Company** is a small private military contractor made up largely of former military personnel who escaped from prison and remained together afterward rather than scattering. Most of its members are still legally wanted in one or more jurisdictions, and the [[United Colonies Security Authority]] publicly classifies the organization as a fugitive military group. Warrant makes little effort to hide that reputation. Its branding openly incorporates prisoner identification numbers, chains, and military insignia, presenting the company less as rehabilitated veterans and more as professionals who already know exactly what governments think of them.

At its largest, Warrant is believed to number **around two hundred personnel**, though only a fraction of that number are conventional combat troops. The company is closer to a collection of specialist teams than a private army. A typical deployment may involve only a squad or two, supported by pilots, drivers, intelligence personnel, communications specialists, technicians, medics, drone operators, or other experts selected for that specific contract.

Its members come from a wide range of military backgrounds. Some were infantry, reconnaissance troops, pilots, vehicle crews, intelligence officers, combat engineers, security specialists, or communications personnel before their imprisonment. Others possess more unusual experience in areas such as surveillance, cyber operations, infiltration, extraction, electronic warfare, or technical support. The result is a company capable of assembling unusually flexible teams from a relatively small pool of people.

Warrant primarily takes **specialized contract work** rather than large security or occupation jobs. Common assignments include armed escort, executive protection, dangerous transport, reconnaissance, recovery operations, infiltration and extraction, site security, hostile-environment work, intelligence gathering, and contracts where a client needs a compact team capable of solving several different problems without bringing in multiple outside companies.

Their reputation is strongest in jobs where mobility matters. Warrant personnel are expected to move people, cargo, or themselves through difficult situations rather than simply hold ground. A contract might require a team to enter unnoticed, locate a person or object, secure it, move it through hostile territory, and then leave using whatever transport is available. The same operation may involve ground vehicles, aircraft, spacecraft, surveillance systems, and direct combat, which is why the company places so much value on personnel who can fill more than one role.

Warrant's equipment is similarly practical. The company uses military weapons, armor, vehicles, aircraft, and spacecraft from a variety of sources, frequently modified by the people operating them. Their distinctive **black, gray, white, and muted orange camouflage** has become closely associated with the company, as have the prisoner-style identification patches worn by many personnel. These markings are not legally required. Warrant adopted them deliberately.

The company does not maintain the infrastructure of a major PMC. It has no large standing army, permanent occupation force, or extensive chain of bases. Most of its assets are mobile, rented, concealed, or operated through sympathetic ports and independent facilities. This keeps Warrant difficult to pin down and allows its personnel to relocate quickly when local authorities become inconvenient.

That mobility is necessary. Warrant personnel remain wanted fugitives, and authorities routinely warn civilians and corporations against hiring, supplying, transporting, or sheltering them. Some governments will arrest members on sight. Others tolerate their presence until someone complains. A few are known to have quietly employed Warrant through intermediaries despite publicly recognizing the same warrants.

For clients, that legal risk is part of the price. Warrant is not hired because it is safe, respectable, or easy to explain afterward. It is hired because its personnel include experienced soldiers, pilots, drivers, intelligence specialists, technicians, and operators who have already survived military service, prison, escape, and life as fugitives.

Their own advertising reduces the company's philosophy to a single line:
**Experience still works.**
